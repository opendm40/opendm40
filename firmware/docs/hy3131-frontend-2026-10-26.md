# HY3131 front end: pre-filter capacitor, AC coupling, AC V compensation switches, DC mV noise (2026-10-26)

One unit (UID 1E6830202422B30736313039), image from branch `exp/hy-poke` with
the debug command `DBG:HY:CAP?` (`src/hyexp.rs` and the host helper
`tools/experiments/hycap.py` there, [PR #13](https://github.com/wirenboard/opendm40/pull/13),
not in main). Source: the
MR9270S+ on V/Ω–COM (DC voltage; the pulse output in its kHz range, a 0 / ~10 V
square up to 100 kHz, frequency checked with the DM40 counter). Data:
[`data/hy-frontend-2026-10-26/`](data/hy-frontend-2026-10-26/).

`DBG:HY:CAP? kind,n,npre{,i,v}` writes the row the DMM last wrote with the
given bytes (i < 22: R20 + i), drops `npre` results, writes the step bytes
(i = 32 + byte) one SPI write each, then returns n results (AD1, RMS or AD2)
with the time since the step. The DMM row is restored afterwards.

## 1. FTP / FTN pre-filter capacitor: ~26 nF

DC V 6 V, calibrator 1 V and 3 V. AD1 at R22 = 0x00 (OSR 256, unchopped,
1.56 ms per result). Step by SPI: R24 0x21 (AD1FP = AD1FN = RLU, zero) ->
0x31 (OP1O - RLU), R33 SFT1 set in the pre row.

| SFT1 | pre-filter R | settling |
|---|---|---|
| 11 | open (capacitor off) | 2 results (the decimation filter) |
| 10 | 0 | 2 results |
| 01 | 10 k | ~1 result more |
| 00 | 100 k | exponential, τ = 2.54..2.59 ms (6 runs, 1 V and 3 V) |

In DS §12.4 (p.27) the 100 k / 10 k resistor is in the AD1FP leg only and the
capacitor sits between FTP (after it) and FTN (on AD1FN), so τ = 100 k · C:
C ≈ 25.6 nF (the on-chip resistor's tolerance is not specified). The Hycon
demo board fits 27 nF (C12, APD-DMM001 p.22).

The slow "memory" of the ohm 6 MΩ / 60 MΩ paths in `DBG:SRC` (SFT1 = 00) is
then the source impedance charging this capacitor (10 MΩ · 26 nF ≈ 0.26 s
against 50 ms conversions) (*inferred*, not measured separately).

## 2. AC V coupling: τ = 0.34 s

AC V 6 V; AD1 on PB4 - AGND (R24 0xC3, SFT1 = 11, R22 = 0x02: OSR 1024
unchopped, 6.25 ms), calibrator DC 0 -> 1 V during the capture. PB4 jumps and
decays as one exponential: τ = 338..347 ms (fit from 0.03..0.6 s after the
peak, residual < 0.1 % of the step). This gives R · C only: the demo board's
1 µF + 1 MΩ would be 1 s; which R and C the DM40 fits is not known.

## 3. AC V compensation switches (FS9, FS1) and the ACC array

The stock AC V rows set FS1 (R2A 0x20) and FS9 + PS8 (R2E 0x28) on every
range; the tap is PS6 / SS6 on 6 V and 60 V (the same tap, OP1 gain differs)
and PS5 / SS5 on 600 V. SMODE5 is set (R2F 0xA0), ACC = 0 (R30 0x80).

RMS results (R09..R0D, √ of 4 results after 4 dropped) of the square with one
change to the row, relative to the stock row, same source level:

| f | 6 V: FS9 off | FS1 off | ACC 127 | 600 V: FS9 off | FS1 off | ACC 127 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 kHz | +0.43 % | +0.02 % | 0.00 % | +0.38 % | -0.02 % | 0.00 % |
| 1 kHz | +0.79 % | +0.03 % | 0.00 % | +0.74 % | -0.01 % | -0.01 % |
| 5 kHz | +3.52 % | +0.20 % | 0.00 % | +3.54 % | +0.08 % | 0.00 % |
| 10 kHz | +7.17 % | +0.45 % | 0.00 % | +7.08 % | +0.24 % | 0.00 % |
| 20 kHz | +17.6 % | +1.8 % | 0.00 % | +17.0 % | +1.1 % | +0.03 % |
| 30 kHz | +29.3 % | +4.4 % | 0.00 % | - | - | - |

(60 V repeats 6 V to 0.1 %. ACC 16 / 64 change nothing either, and ACC 127
with FS9 off moves the FS9-off result by at most 0.3 %; above ~40 kHz the RMS result of this square is at the noise
floor, so higher frequencies say nothing.) The spread of 4 results is
< 0.01 % up to 20 kHz.

- FS9 is in the signal path's frequency response: switched off, the HF
  content rises, by the same amount on the 101 k (6 V) and the 10 k (600 V)
  tap. So its element does not load the divider tap; it sits at a node whose
  impedance does not change with the range (*inferred*).
- The square's fundamental, fitted from raw AD2 samples (reads every ~47 µs,
  snapped to the 13.02 µs conversion grid), rises with FS9 off by +0.46 % at
  2 kHz, +3.0 % at 5 kHz and +11 % at 10 kHz (roughly ∝ f², like a pole near
  20 kHz added by FS9) (`fund_6.csv`; above 10 kHz this fit scatters by 3..25 %).
  With FS9 on the fundamental is -1.5 % at 5 kHz and -10 % at 10 kHz against
  1 kHz, with FS9 off +1.4 % and -0.3 %. That includes the AD2 decimation
  filter, whose response is not documented, so which setting is flatter at
  the input cannot be told from this.
- The ACC array does nothing measurable in the AC V rows (SMODE5 on); where its
  capacitors connect on the DM40 is not known.
- FS1 has a much smaller effect of the same sign (about 1/15 of FS9 at 10 kHz).

Not done: a sine source (GEN to V/Ω–COM), the 750 V range, the DC V rows'
FS0 / DS0.

## 4. DC mV 60 mV: gain stage, reference and noise

The stock DC mV 60 mV row (`CFG_F3[0]`): OP1+ on RLU, OPS1 = 1 (OP1 through
the board's x10 network), AD1 = OP1O - AGND with the input buffers, AD1IG 0.9,
reference PB6 - AGND (the MAX6190), SFT1 = open. Variants written over it with
`DBG:HY:CAP?` (row bytes: R23 3, R24 4, R25 5, R28 8, R31 17, R32 18, R33 19),
AD1 at R22 0x15 (100 ms) or 0x17 (400 ms). Input-referred volts use each
variant's nominal scale, code · VR · RG / IG / 2²³ / G_OP1 (DS §13.1 p.30).

| variant | change | window at the input |
|---|---|---|
| A (stock) | - | ±139 mV |
| B | R25 0x30: OP1 x1, IG 3.6 | ±347 mV |
| C | R24 0x23, R25 0x30, R32 0x32: AD1 = RLU - AGND, OP1 off, IG 3.6 | ±347 mV |
| D | R25 0x11: IG 1.8 | ±69 mV |
| E | R23 0x93: AD1RG 0.333 | ±46 mV |
| F | R23 0x93, R25 0x31: RG 0.333, IG 3.6 | ±11.6 mV |

Noise (sd of one block, median over the blocks, µV at the input). Three

|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1.1..1.25 | 1.1 | 0.41 | 0.22 |
| A int | 0.44 | 0.21 | 0.44 | 0.20 |

| B | 2.9 | 1.3 | - | - |
| B int | 2.1 | 1.0 | 2.0 | 1.1 |

| E / F / G | 1.1 / 1.3 / 1.15 | 1.1 / 1.2 / 0.6 | - | - |

- On the short, A is as quiet as the internal zero, and both halve from 100 to
  400 ms (white). The ~1.1 µV seen on the calibrator does not drop with the
  conversion time: it comes from the calibrator output or its leads, not from
  the meter.
- On the calibrator, IG 1.8, RG 0.333 or the AGNDP6 reference shrink the code
  3..36x and leave the noise in µV unchanged (E, F, G against A on the same
  source); on the short, IG 1.8 does lower it (see the second session below).
  Without x10, AD1 is the floor: B is 2.3..5x A, and the input buffers on RLU
  (C) are noisier still.
- SFT1 100 k or 10 k (the ~26 nF pre-filter, section 1) lowers the shorted
  noise from 0.41 to 0.28 µV at 100 ms and moves the zero by about -4 µV
  (`mv60_blocks.csv`).
- Allan deviation of 300 conversions on the short (`mv60_short_series.csv`):
  A 0.31 µV at 0.1 s, 0.15 µV at 1.6..3.2 s; A int 0.32 µV at 0.1 s, 0.06 µV
  at 3.2 s. The zero of A drifted from 19.7 to 22.6 µV over the 6 min after
  the wire went on (thermal EMF settling, *inferred*); A int stayed at 11.1 µV.

Gain against the nominal scale (no calibration), points 0 / 10 / 30 / 55 mV:
A 1.0028, D 1.0011, B 0.9917, C 0.9917; on ±3 mV: E 0.9986, F 0.9971, G 0.870
(the AGNDP6 tap is 13 % below K2 = 40/120 · REFO; why is not known). All
variants show the same residuals from a line (−11, +9, +9, −6 µV), so they are
the calibrator's level errors, not the meter's. B and C agree to 0.002 %, so
RLU is the node OP1 amplifies in this row (the jack's mV input through the
relay, *inferred*).

The MR9270S+ `millivolt` output: the register value is in 10 µV
(5000 = 50 mV), and it does not go below −10 mV (−30 and −55 mV gave −10.05 mV).

Data: `mv60_blocks.csv` (every block: run, time, variant, R22, set level, n,
mean and sd in µV), `mv60_short_series.csv` (the two 300-conversion series).
Not done: the 600 mV row.

### Pre-filter and IG 1.8 on the calibrator (second session)

Other HY3131 firmwares use SFT1 100 k or 0 and IG 1.8 on their x10 mV range
([comparison](hy3131-firmware-comparison.md#dc-mv)). The same row with R33
0xA0 (100 k) / 0xA8 (0), R25 0x11 (IG 1.8) and both combined, alternating
blocks with stock, calibrator 0 / 10 / 30 / 55 mV
(`mv60_prefilter_blocks.csv`):

| variant | gain / stock | zero − stock |
|---|---|---|
| SFT1 100 k | 1.0000 ± 0.0002 | −5.2 µV |
| SFT1 0 | 1.0000 ± 0.0002 | −6.0 µV |
| IG 1.8 | 0.9984 | −0.1 µV |
| IG 1.8 + 100 k / + 0 | 0.9984 | −3.0 / −4.4 µV |

The pre-filter leaves the gain as it is and shifts the zero by a constant
−5..−6 µV (±2 µV between blocks). IG 1.8 is 0.16 % below twice IG 0.9
(D against A above: 0.9983). The noise on the calibrator was 2.5..3 µV per
100 ms in every variant this time, so it does not separate them.

The same six variants on the shorted jacks, alternating blocks
(`mv60_prefilter_short_blocks.csv`; 3 blocks of 100 at 100 ms, 2 of 30 at
400 ms per variant):

| variant | 100 ms | 400 ms | zero − stock |
|---|---|---|---|
| stock | 0.48 µV | 0.21 µV | - |
| SFT1 100 k | 0.27 | 0.16 | −3.9 µV |
| SFT1 0 | 0.34 | 0.19 | −4.2 µV |
| IG 1.8 | 0.34 | 0.24 | +0.1 µV |
| IG 1.8 + 100 k | 0.18 | 0.09 | −3.8 µV |
| IG 1.8 + 0 | 0.24 | 0.13 | −4.0 µV |

On the short, IG 1.8 lowers the noise as well, and with 100 k the two together
give 2.6x less than stock at 100 ms and 2.2x at 400 ms. So AD1 is part of
the floor with OP1 x10 after all: the E / F / G and D comparisons above were
made on the calibrator, whose own noise hid it. The zero shift of the
pre-filter is −3.8..−4.2 µV, stable to 0.3 µV between blocks.

## 5. Other rows on the shorted jacks

V-COM shorted, calibrator off. Each stock row with byte overrides through
`DBG:HY:CAP?`, variants alternating, 3 blocks each
(`short_rows_blocks.csv`). AD1 ran at the meter's speed setting, R22 = 0x16
(OSR 16384, 200 ms), not 0x15 as in section 4. Input-referred noise = median
block sd × the nominal code weight: 60 mV `1.25 / IG / 10 / 2²³`, 600 mV
`1.25 / IG / 2²³`, 6 V the same × 10 (the divider, `RESEARCH.md` §5).

**AC: sum(XY) against sum(X²).** Stock AC rows run AD3 on the AD2 node with
SVXI = 1; R34 0x88 → 0x80 gives sum(X²) of AD2 alone. Median sqrt(RMS) in
counts:

| row | sum(XY) (stock) | sum(X²) |
|---|---|---|
| AC V 6 V (`CFG_F0` r0) | 19.1 | 48.2 |
| AC mV 60 mV (`CFG_F2` r0) | 9.8 | 44.8 |

sum(XY) lowers the zero floor 2.5x (AC V) and 4.6x (AC mV): the two
converters' own noise averages out, as expected. One block of 15 in each
sum(XY) series had a single result near 10⁹ counts (cause unknown); the
medians leave it out.

**DC 60 mV, choppers** (on IG 1.8 + SFT1 100 k):

| variant | noise | zero |
|---|---|---|
| stock (IG 0.9, SFT1 open) | 0.29 µV | - |
| IG 1.8 + 100 k, OP1CHOP 2 kHz (R33 0xA0) | 0.13 µV | −4 µV vs stock |
| OP1CHOP 1 kHz (0x60) | 0.15 µV | same |
| OP1CHOP off (0x20) | 0.37 µV | +0.4 mV |
| AD1CHOP 11 (R22 0x1D) | 0.17 µV | same |

**DC 600 mV and 6 V** (OP1 x1 on RLU / the 6 V divider tap):

| variant | 600 mV noise | 600 mV zero vs stock | 6 V noise | 6 V zero vs stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stock (IG 0.9, SFT1 open) | 2.2 µV | - | 23 µV | - |
| SFT1 100 k | 1.9 µV | −54 µV | 19 µV | +100 µV |
| IG 1.8 | 1.7 µV | 0 | 16 µV | +3 µV |
| IG 1.8 + 100 k | 1.4 µV | −54 µV | 13 µV | +84 µV |

**Ohm 600 Ω and 60 kΩ** (stock SFT1 = 0, R33 0x68): SFT1 100 k (0x60) and open
(0x6C) give the same noise as stock (54 / 53 / 52 codes on 600 Ω, 22 / 21 / 22
on 60 kΩ) and move the zero by 10..50 codes.

So on every DC voltage row tried, IG 1.8 + SFT1 100 k is 1.6..2.2x quieter than
stock, with a constant zero shift; the choppers are best as stock has them;
the pre-filter makes no difference in ohms; sum(XY) is what keeps the AC floor
low. The gain of IG 1.8 on the 600 mV and 6 V rows was not measured here.

### Can the meter measure the IG 1.8 and pre-filter corrections itself?

Same rows, no input needed (`selfcal_blocks.csv`, 3 rounds of 50 results):
AD1 on an internal tap (R24 0x73: VREF − AGND, R31 SFUVR = 6: VREF = AGNDP6,
~2.86 M codes at IG 0.9), and the internal zero (OP1+ on AGND, R32 0x3B), each
with IG 0.9 / 1.8 and SFT1 open / 100 k.

- IG ratio: code(IG 1.8) / code(IG 0.9) / 2 = 0.99836..0.99838 in all three
  rows, with and without the pre-filter, the same to 1e-5 between rounds. The
  calibrator gave 0.9983..0.9985 through OP1 x10 (section 4). So a firmware
  can measure this factor on the tap and apply it on top of the factory gain.
- Pre-filter zero shift: on the tap it is −291 / −547 codes (IG 0.9 / 1.8) in
  every row, about −46 µV at the AD1 input; on the shorted jacks it was −39
  (60 mV, at OP1O), −54 (600 mV) and +8 µV (6 V, AD1 against RLU); on the
  internal zero it is +350 µV (60 mV), −4 µV (600 mV) and +60 µV (6 V). The
  shift depends on what drives the AD1 input, so the internal zero does not
  predict it; it needs a zero taken on the real input (shorted leads).
